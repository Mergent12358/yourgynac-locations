Skin Infections You Can Show a Dermatologist on Camera

Itchy rashes, suspected fungal infections, and sudden allergic patches are easier to describe when a doctor can see them. A camera is not perfect, but with clear photos and a short history, a dermatologist can often tell you what is likely, what to avoid, and whether you should come in for a closer look.

Take photos in natural light, including a slightly wider shot so the doctor can see the pattern, not just one spot. Mention whether the area is itchy, painful, spreading, or related to a new product, heat, or sweating. If someone else at home has a similar rash, say so.

Do not wait on an online consult if a rash is widespread, painful, blistering, or comes with fever and swelling of the face or lips. Those need prompt in-person care. For many common infections, a first video conversation can still be a calm and practical starting point.
